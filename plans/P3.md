## 3. Design decisions (D1–D4, D7 at the P3a stop; D5, D6, D8–D10 at a stop opening P3b)

Each has a recommended default. Measure where the default needs evidence.

### D1 — Elevation

- **Question:** add a terrain model now? Bristol's gradients change walking catchments
  and cycle times materially (Totterdown, Kingsdown, Clifton).
- **How:** a DEM raster for the clip box. Candidates (rule 3 — navigate and confirm
  licence and resolution first): Environment Agency LiDAR Composite DTM (England);
  Natural Resources Wales LiDAR (Newport, Monmouthshire); OS Terrain 50 as a coarse
  fallback. Resample to a resolution R5 handles comfortably; record it.
- **Engines:** r5r reads a `.tif` in the network folder (`build_network(...,
  elevation = "TOBLER")`, or `"MINETTI"`). Check whether r5py 1.1.7's
  `TransportNetwork` accepts an elevation model; if it doesn't, decide whether walk and
  cycle skims move to r5r. Both engines must use the same DEM and cost function.
  **Checked (2026-10-09): it does.** r5py 1.1.7's `TransportNetwork(osm_pbf, gtfs,
  elevation_model=None, elevation_cost_function=ElevationCostFunction.TOBLER)` takes
  one or more single-band GeoTIFFs in metres (`MINETTI` is the other function), and
  `rasterio` 1.5.1 is already in the env. So walk and cycle skims stay on r5py. Read
  from the installed code, not run: the spike must still show that the same DEM gives
  the same walk times in r5py and r5r, and how R5 applies the cost to walking as
  against cycling. `pt_skims.R` calls `build_network(cfg$net_dir, verbose = FALSE)`,
  so the r5r side is one argument plus the `.tif` in the network folder; the DEM hash
  joins the network and chunk cache keys.
- **Effect:** every walk, cycle and PT skim changes. Re-run the r5r vs r5py regression,
  the PT spot checks (the P2 score of 15 and 18 of 20 was made on flat walking) and the
  DfT comparison; report changes against P2 outputs (P2 stays closed; this is
  a new baseline version, `B2026` network v2, recorded in the run record).
- **Recommendation:** add it in P3a, **before any scenario baseline is stored**, so
  every scenario comparison uses one consistent network. Spike first on a small area:
  measure the walk-time change on steep vs flat OAs.

### D2 — Distance decay by mode

- Fit a decay curve per mode class to observed walk-to-stop distances. Candidate
  figures, **unverified** (seen second-hand in a planning document citing WYG, "How far
  is it acceptable to walk?" (2018), from NTS 2014): walk to bus mean 580 m, 85th
  percentile 800 m; walk to rail mean 1,010 m, 85th percentile 1,610 m. Find and open
  the primary source before tagging `[SOURCED]`; until then `[PLACEHOLDER]`.
- Functional form: logistic or negative exponential, fitted to the mean and 85th
  percentile; pick on fit and record why.
- Mode classes: bus, BRT (Metrobus by route ID in config), tram/light rail, heavy rail
  and metro. Tram and BRT interpolated between bus and rail `[MODELLED]`, with a
  sensitivity run at each end.
- **Distance is walking time on the network** (D1 applies), converted with the walk
  speed, not straight-line distance.
- A **reduced-mobility variant** (shorter decay, e.g. a 400 m half-weight point)
  `[MODELLED]`, reported alongside, for equity.

### D3 — Service-quality score

- Per OA and period: combine decay-weighted access to each stop with that stop's
  service level. Options: (a) discrete classes on the Swiss ARE "ÖV-Güteklassen" model
  (stop category from mode and headway × walking-distance band) — navigate to ARE's
  method document first; (b) a continuous score (Σ over stop clusters of decay ×
  departures per hour, capped per direction). Recommendation: (a) for publication
  (legible), (b) stored for analysis.
- **Frequent** threshold: SPEC said ≤ 10 min; the scenarios discussed use 15 min.
  Make it a parameter; report both.
- Count each **stop cluster** once (D4), and departures per cluster in distinct
  directions, so a stop served by four routes on one road isn't counted as four.
- **Periods are not yet defined** (repo check; §8 Q6): skims use single hours (08–09,
  12–13), demand uses 07–10 / 10–16 / 16–19. The feeds cover the whole day.

### D4 — Stop clusters

- Group stops into clusters (rail: one per station; bus: same name or within a radius
  `[MODELLED]`, both sides of the road). Store the mapping; test that no cluster spans
  a river or railway barrier by accident (walking-distance check between members).

### D5 — Rail-restructuring ops

SPEC §5's ops cover new lines and headway changes but not changing existing services.
Proposed:
- `modify_route` extended: `stopping_pattern` (add or remove calls), `extend_to`,
  `truncate_at`.
- `replace_route`: remove a route's trips in a period and add a generated route that
  takes its place, keeping a link for reporting.
- `reroute`: a route follows a new alignment between two stops (e.g. into a tunnel),
  run times from D8.
All ops fail loudly on unknown IDs, stops off the alignment, or a reroute whose ends
aren't on the route. Route IDs and station codes stay in `scenarios/` (rule 6).

### D6 — Track-capacity check

- **Sections** as data (`scenarios/infrastructure/*.yaml` or config): ordered TIPLOC
  pairs with track count and a max trains per hour per direction `[MODELLED]`, plus
  junctions flagged as flat or grade-separated.
- **Base usage from Darwin:** the converter currently drops passing points (`PP`)
  (confirmed: `gtfs_rail.OPERATIONAL`; the snapshot does carry them, with `wtp`).
  **Limits found at the repo check (§8 Q4):** Darwin holds no freight; the converter
  also drops non-passenger journeys (7,456 on the date, mostly empty stock) and
  journeys with fewer than two calls in the clip box, all of which use track. The side
  table must be built from every journey before those filters, and freight paths stay
  uncounted unless a source is found.
  Keep them in a side table, so each base train's path through junctions (e.g. which
  way it leaves Temple Meads) is known, and count tph per section per period.
- **Check:** base + scenario tph per section ≤ limit; flat junctions with conflicting
  moves above a threshold are flagged, not failed. Report a section table per run.
- State plainly in SPEC §11: this catches overloads; it does not prove a timetable.

### D7 — Timing of generated services

- The base is timetable-based; R5 randomises `frequencies.txt` services. Options:
  (a) `frequencies.txt` with a deliberate `draws_per_minute` (tagged); (b) explicit
  generated trips (`stop_times.txt`) at the stated headway with a fixed offset, plus
  an offset-sensitivity run. Measure both on one test line (difference in p50 and
  mean wait against the base-style timetable). Recommendation: (b) unless (a)
  matches within 1 min p50 on ≥ 99% of pairs.

### D8 — Run times on existing track

- For segments with `alignment_type: existing_rail`, take sectional running times from
  the base Darwin schedules (working times between TIPLOCs for stopping services of the
  same traction class), plus dwell `[MODELLED]`. Confirmed: the snapshot carries
  `wta` / `wtd` / `wtp` on calling and passing points; the converter reads only
  `pta` / `ptd`, so this needs a second pass over the XML, not the GTFS. Darwin gives
  no traction class (only `toc`, `trainCat`, `trainId`): "same traction class" has to
  come from data in `scenarios/` or config, or be dropped (§8 Q5). Use the geometry + speed-profile rule
  only for new alignment (tunnel, new chords). Record which rule each segment used.
- Electric vs diesel/bi-mode acceleration: a traction parameter per generated route,
  `[MODELLED]` until sourced.

### D9 — Cost library structure

- **Price base year** chosen once; every entry deflated to it (index named and
  sourced).
- **Stop cost by alignment type** (at-grade stop, rail platform, reopened station,
  cut-and-cover station, mined/deep station). Underground stations dominate heavy-rail
  tunnel costs, so a single `stop_cost[mode]` is not enough.
- **Heavy-rail items:** electrification per single-track km (simple and complex),
  track doubling per km, grade-separated junction (each), bored tunnel per km (single
  and twin bore), portal (each), bay platform (each), fleet per vehicle by type,
  depot.
- **Tram:** the current seed (£20–30m per route-km incl. vehicles) is well below recent
  UK outturns and would understate tram costs several-fold. Replace it with sourced
  outturns (e.g. recent Edinburgh, Manchester and West Midlands extensions — confirm
  figures from primary documents) and the Bristol mass transit studies' own unit
  costs, per km by alignment type, with ranges.
- **Optimism bias:** TAG A1.2 uplift by scheme type and stage; report raw and uplifted.
- Every value: `value`, `range`, `unit`, `price_year`, `source`, `tag`. Figures from
  chat discussions are not sources.

rule from P2; record the evidence either way.

**Recommendations.**

- **D1 — elevation: adopt it, with Tobler, on OS Terrain 50, as network v2 — but
  decide the level question first.** Evidence: walking +6.6% overall and +10.6% from the
  steepest fifth of OAs; Minetti doubles that (+13.9%, +20.0%); engines agree exactly.
  Two reservations. (1) R5's terrain cost never speeds anything up, so even flat areas
  are 3% slower: adopting it lowers the effective walk speed below the sourced 4.8 km/h
  everywhere. (2) One function serves both walking and cycling; Minetti is an energy
  function and its +15% on cycling has no observed target here. Tobler is the smaller
  departure from the sourced speeds. Per Q2 the PT spot checks are re-scored on v2
  before it is adopted (P3b-0); two or more extra failures means looking at the cost
  function first. Not tested: whether a finer DEM changes the result.
- **D2 — decay: logistic, per mode class, on walking time.** It fits both observed
  statistics exactly where an exponential cannot. BRT a third of the way from bus to
  rail, tram two-thirds, `[MODELLED]`, with the end-point sensitivity reported
  (BRT: 588,017–612,594 residents at 10 min). **Blocked on the source**: nothing built
  on these curves is publishable until the WYG paper (or another primary source for
  walk-to-stop distances) is opened.
- **D3 — score: publish the frequent-service flag and the ARE class; keep the continuous
  score for analysis.** Headline = the worse of AM and inter-peak, departures in one
  direction (the ARE halving, with termini and one-way stops not halved). Note the ARE
  class uses its own distance bands (to 1 km, any mode), not the decay curves, and the
  network distance in place of ARE's straight line: stricter than the Swiss original.
  The 10 / 15 minute choice is yours: 48.6% against 66.9% of residents.
- **D4 — clusters: as built** (same name within 150 m, any name within 40 m, cut where
  the network walk exceeds 4 min). 17 pairs cut; some look like walk-network faults.
- **D7 — explicit trips, not `frequencies.txt`.** Frequency-based routing matched the
  timetable within 1 minute on 37–43% of the pairs that use the route, against the

  offset is a recorded, tagged input of the scenario.
