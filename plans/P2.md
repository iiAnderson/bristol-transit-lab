### D3 — Routing points and skim grain

- **Origins:** OA population-weighted centroids inside the extent (**3,799**). Also
  used for OA accessibility.
- **Destinations:** LSOA points (**729**). Default: the LSOA population-weighted
  centroid (already in `lsoa_pwc` / `int_lsoa`).
  - Measure in the spike whether a job-weighted LSOA point changes HBW times materially.
    If it does, use it for HBW destinations.
- **Why this grain:** routing OA → LSOA (3,799 × 729 ≈ 2.77M pairs per mode and
  period) serves both the LSOA → LSOA skims and OA-level accessibility.
- **LSOA → LSOA skims:** origin OAs aggregated with population weights. Compute GC per OA
  pair first, then take the weighted mean (not the GC of mean times).
- **Runtime:** estimate the full PT skim cost (with the 60-minute window and
  percentiles) from a 1% sample, as upstream did for OSRM, before committing to it.

- **Approved grain at the P2a stop, with a correction:** job-weighted LSOA points *can* be
  built from Census 2021 workplace counts at OA (destination totals of the national
  ODWP01EW OA file, in upstream's raw data). Lockdown distorts the level, but the
  within-LSOA distribution is usable (log that). Measure the change in HBW PT and car
  times against the PWC points. If the median absolute change is > 2 min or > 5%
  [MODELLED thresholds], use job-weighted points for HBW destinations; otherwise keep
  PWCs. Log the result either way.

### D7 — PT cost components (new)

- **Finding:** r5py 1.1.7's `TravelTimeMatrix` returns only total travel time at the
  requested percentiles (`travel_time_p25/p50/p75`). There is no walk / wait / IVT /
  transfer breakdown. `DetailedItineraries` returns legs, but for single departure
  times, and it is much slower.
- **Proposed default:**
  - total times from `TravelTimeMatrix` at OA → LSOA, 60-minute window,
    p25/p50/p75;
  - components from `DetailedItineraries` on LSOA-PWC → LSOA-PWC pairs (729² ≈ 531k),
    at a small number of departure times spread across the window (e.g. 3), for the
    fastest itinerary at each;
  - GC per pair = total p50 + (w_walk − 1)·walk + (w_wait − 1)·wait +
    n_transfers·P_interchange, using the component mix from the itinerary sample.
    This is an approximation, and it's recorded as one.
- **Alternatives, if A8 shows the itinerary run is too slow:** components for a
  stratified sample of pairs only, with a fitted model of components against total time
  and distance; or calling R5's Java API directly for path details (more work, and it
  bypasses r5py). Decide on the A8 measurements.

- **Not approved at the P2a stop.** Investigate, in order, and **stop to report before
  committing to a method or building skims on it**:
  1. **r5r `expanded_travel_time_matrix()`** (access / wait / ride / transfer / egress per
     departure minute). Check compatibility with the R5 version in use (a separate
     r5r-pinned R5 jar is acceptable if recorded); measure runtime on the D3 1% sample.
     (Note: R is *not* in the toolchain — UK2GTFS was dropped when rail moved to Darwin.
     Adding R is part of this check.)
  2. **Components only where needed:** accessibility needs only totals; components are
     needed only for LSOA → LSOA pairs with HBW demand. Count them and project exact
     components for them with the chosen method.
  **Findings (2026-09-27):**
  - Step 1 **feasible**: r5r 2.4.0 (CRAN) pins R5 **7.5.1**, the same R5 version as
    r5py 1.1.7; runs on JDK 21 in a separate env (`environment-r.yml`). On the D3 1%
    sample (38 OAs × 729 LSOAs, 60-min window, every departure minute) the expanded
    matrix takes **6.2 s** → full OA → LSOA ~**10 min per period**. Output per minute:
    access, wait, ride, transfer, egress, n_rides, routes, total.
  - **Agreement with r5py** (same network, `max_rides = 8` to match r5py): the same pairs
    are reachable at p50 (20,728 vs 20,729); r5r − r5py p50 median +0.5 min, p5–p95
    0.0–0.9, 99.6% within 1 min (decimal vs whole minutes). r5r's default `max_rides = 3`
    must be overridden.
  - Components sum to the total within 0.05 min on 96.7% of rows; the other 3.3% are all
    walk-only (`n_rides = 0`, components 0, walk = total).
  - Step 2: 232,388 LSOA → LSOA pairs carry internal HBW demand (44% of 531k); 52,544
    carry ≥ 1 trip (82% of trips). Not needed as a restriction: step 1 is cheap enough
    for every pair.
  - Step 3 (fitted model): not needed.
  - **Open for approval:** (a) PT skims from r5r's expanded matrix (totals p25/p50/p75 and
    components from one per-minute output) vs r5py totals + r5r components; (b) how to
    reduce 60 departure minutes to one GC per OD — GC per minute then the mean (or
    median) over minutes, vs components at the median-total minute; (c) per-minute output
    is ~450 MB per period uncompressed-equivalent, so aggregate in R per origin chunk and
    keep only the OD summary.
  **Approved (Robbie, 2026-09-27):**
  - (a) r5r for PT totals and components from the one per-minute output; r5py for walk
    and cycle. The 1% r5r-vs-r5py comparison becomes a regression test (same reachable
    pairs ± 0.1%; ≥ 99% of p50 within 1 min). Every routing setting both engines use
    lives in `params/base.yaml`, tagged, passed to both (max rides 8, walk and cycle
    speeds, max walk/access time, max trip duration, departure window).
  - (b) Mean over departure minutes; store mean components, compute GC in Python from
    `params/` (weights change without re-running r5r). Test: GC from stored means =
    mean of per-minute GC on the 1% sample.
  - (c) Aggregate in R per origin chunk; keep the full per-minute output only for the 1%
    sample (audit, tests).
  - Per pair and period store: mean access/wait/ride/transfer/egress, mean n_rides,
    p25/p50/p75 total, best-minute total, reachable share, walk-only share, top-3
    routes. Walk-only minutes: walk = total. Unreachable if < 50% of minutes reachable
    [MODELLED, params]; else means over reachable minutes. OA → LSOA → LSOA in Python
    with population weights (SPEC §4).
  - PT skims are built in **P2c**, after the P2b stop.
  3. **Fitted model only if 1 and 2 both exceed 48 h of compute:** stratified sample,
     validated on a held-out sample, GC error distribution reported, carried as a flagged
     limitation on every output that uses it.

## 5. P2b — Congestion calibration and validation

### Model

```
car link time = free-flow time × factor[period][direction][road_class][area_type]
```

- Periods: AM and IP now, with the PM parameters fitted but unused.
- **The calibration target is absolute speed.** DfT reports average speeds, so fit
  modelled speeds to them directly; the factor is how the fit is represented.
- **Free-flow speed:** OSM `maxspeed` where tagged, else the defaults of the chosen
  engine. Record the share of links with tagged limits.

### Data processing

**B1 — Bus-lane and busway exclusion list.**
- Ways tagged `highway=busway`, `busway=*`, `bus:lanes=*`, `lanes:bus=*`,
  `lanes:psv=*`, `psv=designated` lanes.
- Plus the Metrobus guided and segregated sections: Metrobus services are identified by
  the route IDs in config, and their shapes are matched to OSM.
- Also flag a link if it is part-lane, since part of its length may still be shared.
- Plot the list for review.

**B2 — Vehicle-location processing.**
- **Map-matching:** match positions to OSM ways (OSRM `/match`, or a lightweight HMM
  matcher, decided on quality).
- **Speeds:** compute moving speeds per way traversal. Drop positions within
  `stop_buffer_m` of a GTFS stop and positions below `min_moving_kmh`.
- **Exclusions:** drop excluded ways (B1) and part-lane links.
- **Aggregation:** by way × direction × period over neutral weekdays. Keep a way only if
  it has at least `min_obs_per_link` traversals.
- **Report:**
  - coverage by road class and area type;
  - the share of each local highway authority's A-road length covered by kept bus
    data.

**B3 — WebTRIS processing.** Mean speed by site × direction × period, flow-weighted
within period. Map each site to its SRN way section.

### Fit

- **Motorway/trunk:** fitted directly to WebTRIS by period and direction. Where a
  section has no site, interpolate between neighbouring sites along the same road;
  otherwise use the class mean.
- **Local A roads:**
  - **Constraint:** fit so each local highway authority's modelled, flow-weighted
    (AADF) average local-A-road speed matches the DfT figure for each period and the
    urban/rural split. Compute it like-for-like with DfT's own definition. (If A7 finds
    the table is coarser, the constraint uses whatever grain DfT publishes, and bus
    data carries more of the period/area split. Decided at the P2a stop.)
  - **Shape:** filtered bus moving speeds shape the relative pattern between area types
    and directions *within* each authority, as a soft term.
- **Minor roads (tertiary and below):** bus-derived where coverage exists. Elsewhere,
  a `[MODELLED]` ratio to the local A-road factor for the same area type, direction and
  period, with the reasoning recorded.
- **Wales:** transferred factors (D8).
- **Parsimony:** keep the parameter count well below the number of independent targets,
  and regularise towards the class mean where data is thin. Report the count.
- **Recovery test:** fitted parameters are written to `params/` as `[CALIBRATED]`, with
  target and fit. Before that, a synthetic test must recover known factors.

### Build car skims

- Apply the fitted speeds in the engine chosen in D2.
- Produce AM and IP car times for the D3 point sets.
- **Car GC in P2** = in-vehicle time + parking search + access walk, all by destination
  area type (`[PLACEHOLDER]` until sourced; flagged in outputs).

### Validation on held-out data

- **Hold-out design:** calibration uses DfT and WebTRIS only. Bus data is used for shape
  on a **spatially blocked** fit set: hold out whole corridors, not random links, to
  avoid leakage.
- **Validate against:**
  - held-out bus moving speeds;
  - Historic journey times, if usable (relative pattern only if pre-2020);
  - ATC speeds, when they arrive (as a re-run: `lab congestion calibrate --validate`
    reads an optional ATC input, so adding it later changes no code).
- **Report distributions, not just means:** median, p10 and p90 of modelled ÷ observed,
  by area type, road class and period. Include a map of residuals.
- **Car spot checks:** 20 OD pairs spanning centre, radial, orbital and inter-urban
  routes (e.g. Bath → Bristol), compared against observed route times from the held-out
  sources where they exist.
- **Timetable vs observed:** a report of GTFS scheduled bus running times against
  observed ones, by route and period. It's informational and feeds the §12 reliability
  item. Timetables are not changed in v1.

### Outputs

- A `link_speed(period, osm_way_id, direction, speed_kmh, factor, source, tag)` table.
- Car skims per period.
- A congestion-factor map (AM, centre vs suburbs) as GeoParquet and PNG. It's a
  candidate blog graphic.
- A calibration and validation report in `runs/<run_id>/`.

### Acceptance (P2b stop)

Tolerances are `[MODELLED]` starting points.
- Each authority × period local-A-road modelled speed is within ±5% of DfT (at the
  grain DfT publishes).
- SRN period-direction speeds are within ±10% of WebTRIS at site level, median.
- Held-out bus moving-speed error: median |error| ≤ 15%, with the distribution reported.
- Car spot checks: at least 16 of 20 within ±15%. Each failure is explained.
- The synthetic recovery test passes.
- The parameter count and regularisation are reported.
